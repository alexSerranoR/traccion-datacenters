\begin{letter}{The Editor-in-Chief\\ \textit{Applied Energy}}
\opening{Dear Editor,}

We submit for your consideration the manuscript ``Hosting flexible data centres on the grid-connection capacity of high-speed railway traction substations: hosting capacity, implementation architecture and security for two Spanish lines''.

Access to the grid has become the binding constraint for new data centres in Europe. At the same time, high-speed railway traction substations hold transmission-level access permits that they use, on average, at 6--10\,\% of their rating. To our knowledge this is the first study that quantifies how much data-centre load could share that capacity with absolute priority for the train, using the access capacity actually granted to each railway bay and a timetable-driven, second-by-second simulation of two Spanish lines of contrasting traffic density.

The manuscript makes four contributions that we believe fit the scope of \textit{Applied Energy}: (i) an open and reproducible hosting-capacity model, in which every figure quoted in the text is generated from the published result tables; (ii) the finding that a shared access permit would unlock about 400\,MW per line with power batteries, and that the averaging window used to enforce the joint limit is the most influential parameter; (iii) an implementation architecture built from standard equipment, with its cost and a staged pilot; and (iv) a security analysis that translates recent railway and grid incidents into design requirements, so that the railway acquires no new failure mode. The paper also quantifies how the same flexibility lowers the cost of hourly renewable matching required by the forthcoming Spanish data-centre regulation.

The manuscript is original, has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission. The limitations of the study, in particular the use of simulated rather than measured substation loads, are stated explicitly.

\closing{Yours faithfully,}
